Anatomy of an AI agent

What Actually Happens Inside an Agentic System

An agent works toward a goal in a loop. Each cycle is bounded by the tools it may use, the knowledge it may read, the rules it must follow and the people who approve its work.

  1. PerceiveReads the trigger: a message, a form, a document or a change in a system.
  2. ReasonWorks out what is being asked and plans the next step toward the goal.
  3. ActUses only the tools it has been given: search knowledge, update a record, draft a reply.
  4. ObserveChecks the result, then continues, finishes or hands the work to a person.
  • ToolsThe specific systems and actions the agent may use, nothing more.
  • KnowledgeApproved documents and data it may read from.
  • MemoryWhat it keeps between steps, and what it must not retain.
  • GuardrailsBusiness rules and limits that stop or redirect it.
  • Human oversightNamed people who approve, correct and own the outcome.

    Agentic AI, explained

    Common Questions About AI Agents and Automation

    What is agentic AI for business?

    Agentic AI refers to systems that pursue a defined goal across several steps. They retrieve information, choose among allowed actions, use business tools such as a CRM or calendar, and hand off to a person when judgment is needed. Unlike a chatbot, which answers a question, an agentic system completes bounded work inside rules the business sets.

    How is agentic AI different from automation?

    Traditional automation follows fixed rules: when X happens, do Y. It is fast, cheap and predictable, and it is often the right choice. Agentic AI can interpret unstructured information and choose between actions, which helps when inputs vary. That flexibility brings more risk, so it needs tighter limits, testing and human review.

    What is the difference between an AI assistant and an AI agent?

    An AI assistant helps a person do a task: drafting, summarizing or finding information, with the person deciding what happens next. An AI agent takes actions itself within defined limits, such as updating a record or routing a request. Assistants keep a person in every loop; agents need explicit rules about what they may do alone.

    When should a business use an AI agent?

    Use an AI agent when a workflow is frequent, involves several steps or systems, needs some interpretation of varied inputs, and has errors that can be caught before they cause harm. If the steps are fixed and predictable, simple automation is usually cheaper and more reliable.

    What processes should not be automated?

    Avoid automating processes that are not yet defined, that change constantly, that are rare, or where a mistake would be serious and hard to reverse. Decisions involving sensitive personal information, professional judgment, or relationships that depend on trust should stay with people, with AI supporting rather than replacing them.
